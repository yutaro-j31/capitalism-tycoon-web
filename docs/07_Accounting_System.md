# 07 Accounting System

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the accounting model, journal rules, financial statements, reconciliation requirements, and financial integrity constraints for Capitalism Tycoon Web.

Accounting is not a presentation layer. It is the authoritative record of financial consequences generated by the simulation.

## 2. Core Principles

1. Double-entry bookkeeping is the conceptual basis.
2. Company and personal books are separate ledgers.
3. Cash movement requires corresponding accounting entries.
4. Profit is not the same as cash flow.
5. Balance-sheet values must reconcile after every committed week.
6. Reports are derived from ledger data or documented opening balances.
7. No UI component may mutate accounting state directly.

## 3. Canonical Money Representation

All monetary state must use one canonical representation.

Recommended approach:

```ts
type Money = number // integer in smallest supported unit
```

If Japanese yen is the canonical game currency and fractional yen is not modeled, `Money` is an integer number of yen.

Rules:

- Do not persist floating-point fractions of currency.
- Apply explicit rounding at transaction boundaries.
- Do not use formatted strings as values.
- Reject `NaN`, `Infinity`, and unsafe integers.
- Large-value limits must be tested for long-duration play.

## 4. Chart of Accounts

Minimum company account classes:

### Assets

- Cash and cash equivalents.
- Accounts receivable.
- Inventory.
- Prepaid expenses.
- Property and equipment.
- Accumulated depreciation as contra-asset.
- Investments.
- Goodwill and identifiable intangibles where modeled.
- Deferred tax assets where modeled.

### Liabilities

- Accounts payable.
- Accrued payroll and expenses.
- Taxes payable.
- Short-term debt.
- Long-term debt.
- Lease liabilities where modeled.
- Deferred revenue.

### Equity

- Common stock or contributed capital.
- Additional paid-in capital where modeled.
- Retained earnings.
- Treasury stock as contra-equity.
- Current-period income closing account if used internally.

### Revenue

- Product sales.
- Service revenue.
- Franchise or licensing revenue.
- Investment income.
- Other operating revenue.

### Expenses

- Cost of goods sold.
- Payroll.
- Rent.
- Advertising.
- Utilities.
- Maintenance.
- Depreciation and amortization.
- Research and development.
- General and administrative expense.
- Interest expense.
- Tax expense.
- Impairment and loss categories.

## 5. Journal Entry Model

```ts
interface JournalEntry {
  id: TransactionId
  week: number
  entityId: CompanyId | PlayerId
  eventType: string
  description: string
  lines: JournalLine[]
  sourceReference?: string
  idempotencyKey?: string
}

interface JournalLine {
  accountId: AccountId
  debit: Money
  credit: Money
  counterpartyId?: string
}
```

Entry invariant:

```text
sum(debits) = sum(credits)
```

A line must not contain both a positive debit and positive credit.

## 6. Idempotency

Recurring and boundary processes must avoid duplicate posting.

Examples:

- Weekly payroll.
- Monthly or quarterly interest.
- Tax close.
- Dividend payment.
- M&A settlement.
- Loan origination.

Each posting should use a stable idempotency key such as:

```text
payroll:{companyId}:{week}
interest:{loanId}:{week}
dividend:{declarationId}:payment
```

A retry must return or recognize the existing posting rather than duplicating it.

## 7. Revenue Recognition

Restaurant and retail revenue is generally recognized when the sale is fulfilled.

Typical cash sale:

```text
Debit: Cash
Credit: Sales Revenue
```

Related inventory consumption:

```text
Debit: Cost of Goods Sold
Credit: Inventory
```

If the simulation does not track physical inventory for a product category, direct weekly input expense may be used, but the policy must be consistent and documented.

Unfulfilled demand is not revenue.

## 8. Operating Expenses

Expenses are recognized in the period in which the economic service is consumed.

Examples:

- Weekly payroll for work performed.
- Weekly rent allocation.
- Advertising expense or prepaid allocation.
- Maintenance expense when performed.
- Utility expense based on operations.

Cash payment timing may differ, creating payable, accrued, or prepaid balances.

## 9. Capital Expenditure and Depreciation

A long-lived asset purchase is not immediately treated as full operating expense unless the feature explicitly models a simplified policy.

Purchase:

```text
Debit: Property and Equipment
Credit: Cash or Accounts Payable
```

Periodic depreciation:

```text
Debit: Depreciation Expense
Credit: Accumulated Depreciation
```

Depreciation method, useful life, residual value, and impairment rules must be data-driven where practical.

## 10. Debt and Interest

Loan origination:

```text
Debit: Cash
Credit: Debt Liability
```

Principal repayment:

```text
Debit: Debt Liability
Credit: Cash
```

Interest payment:

```text
Debit: Interest Expense or Accrued Interest
Credit: Cash
```

Principal repayment is not an expense. Interest is not a reduction of principal.

Interest calculation must define:

- Principal basis.
- Annual or weekly rate.
- Compounding convention.
- Payment frequency.
- Rounding.
- Delinquency treatment.

## 11. Equity Financing

Share issuance:

```text
Debit: Cash
Credit: Contributed Capital / Share Capital
```

Issuance changes capitalization-table shares and accounting equity in the same transaction boundary.

Share repurchase:

```text
Debit: Treasury Stock or Equity Reduction
Credit: Cash
```

A repurchase must not be recorded as an operating expense.

## 12. Salary and Dividends

Salary is compensation expense to the company and income/cash receipt to the player.

Company:

```text
Debit: Compensation Expense
Credit: Cash or Compensation Payable
```

Player:

```text
Debit: Personal Cash
Credit: Personal Employment Income or Equity
```

Dividend declaration and payment must be distinct if both stages are modeled.

A dividend is a distribution of equity, not a company expense.

## 13. Taxes

Tax design may be simplified, but must remain internally consistent.

Define separately:

- Corporate income tax.
- Consumption or sales tax if modeled.
- Payroll-related tax if modeled.
- Personal income tax.
- Capital-gains tax.
- Property tax.

Tax expense, tax payable, and cash tax payment are distinct concepts.

A tax-rate change must not retroactively alter closed periods unless an explicit restatement or migration is performed.

## 14. Financial Statements

### 14.1 Income Statement

```text
Revenue
- Cost of Goods Sold
= Gross Profit
- Operating Expenses
= Operating Profit
+/- Non-operating Income and Expense
= Pretax Profit
- Tax Expense
= Net Income
```

### 14.2 Balance Sheet

```text
Assets = Liabilities + Equity
```

This equation must hold after each committed week, subject only to explicitly documented rounding tolerances. With integer currency, expected tolerance is zero.

### 14.3 Cash Flow Statement

Cash flow categories:

- Operating activities.
- Investing activities.
- Financing activities.

Opening cash plus net cash movement must equal closing cash.

## 15. Retained Earnings

At period close:

```text
Ending Retained Earnings
= Beginning Retained Earnings
+ Net Income
- Dividends
+/- prior-period adjustments if explicitly modeled
```

Current-period profit must not be counted twice in equity.

## 16. Consolidation

When a parent controls subsidiaries and consolidated statements are shown:

- Intercompany receivables and payables are eliminated.
- Intercompany revenue and expense are eliminated.
- Parent investment and subsidiary equity are treated according to the simplified consolidation model.
- Non-controlling interests are shown where applicable.

Until full consolidation is implemented, UI must clearly label standalone versus group values.

## 17. M&A Accounting

The transaction model must distinguish share acquisition from asset acquisition.

Potential components:

- Consideration paid.
- Acquired identifiable assets.
- Assumed liabilities.
- Goodwill.
- Bargain purchase gain if modeled.
- Transaction fees.
- Financing fees.

The game may simplify purchase accounting, but may not add the purchase price as both an asset and expense.

## 18. Insolvency and Bankruptcy

Liquidity failure and balance-sheet insolvency are separate signals.

Possible tests:

- Cash unable to meet due obligations.
- Negative equity.
- Covenant violation.
- Persistent operating losses.
- Credit unavailable.

Bankruptcy processing must post settlements, write-downs, asset disposals, debt forgiveness, and shareholder recovery through explicit entries.

## 19. Reporting Metrics

Derived metrics must identify source periods and denominators.

Examples:

- Gross margin.
- Operating margin.
- Net margin.
- Return on assets.
- Return on equity.
- Debt-to-equity.
- Interest coverage.
- Free cash flow.
- Same-store sales.
- Earnings per share.

Do not persist a derived metric as a second source of truth unless required for historical snapshots.

## 20. Reconciliation Pipeline

After financial posting:

1. Validate each journal entry balances.
2. Recalculate account balances.
3. Reconcile cash accounts to cash ledger.
4. Verify balance-sheet equation.
5. Reconcile retained earnings.
6. Verify debt balances against debt instruments.
7. Verify share-capital entries against capitalization table.
8. Verify player/company transfer pairs.
9. Reject non-finite or unsafe values.
10. Produce diagnostic details on failure.

## 21. Error Handling

A failed accounting invariant blocks turn commit.

Error reports should include:

- Week.
- Entity ID.
- Invariant name.
- Relevant accounts.
- Related journal entries.
- Expected and actual values.

The engine must not silently force the balance sheet to balance through an unexplained adjustment account.

## 22. Required Tests

1. Cash sale and cost-of-goods posting.
2. Payroll accrual and payment.
3. Capital expenditure and depreciation.
4. Loan origination, interest, and principal repayment.
5. Equity issuance and dilution reconciliation.
6. Salary transfer between company and player.
7. Dividend treatment as equity distribution.
8. Income statement to retained-earnings roll-forward.
9. Cash-flow reconciliation.
10. Balance-sheet equation after every simulated week.
11. Duplicate idempotency-key rejection.
12. Quarter and annual close.
13. Bankruptcy settlement.
14. 1,200-week non-finite-value regression.

## 23. Change Control

Any change affecting transaction classification, statement formulas, rounding, tax, debt, equity, transfer rules, or consolidation must update this document and include reconciliation tests.
